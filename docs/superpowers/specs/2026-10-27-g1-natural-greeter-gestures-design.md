# G1 223 natural greeter gestures (ARDY clips) — design

Date: 2026-10-27. Status: approved by the user 2026-10-27; two corrections from planning are marked
in item 4 and item 5.

## Intent

The greeter (`python -m dimos.robot.unitree.g1.greeter`) loops G1 223 through four acts: invite,
take_right, entrance_right, wave. They look robotic. The user's main complaint is the wave: "the
wave swing is slow and slight awkward."

The cause is in the engine, not just the keyframes. `keyframe_plan`
(`dimos/robot/unitree/g1/sonic_arm_reference.py`) turns every keyframe into its own min-jerk stroke
that starts and ends at rest, lasts at least `move_s` (2.0 s for the left-arm gestures) and keeps
every joint under `PEAK_SPEED` (0.75 rad/s). The wave's six ±0.35 rad shoulder-yaw swings therefore
run at ~0.25 Hz and stop dead at both ends. A person waves at ~1.5-2 Hz.

The fix has two parts. First, replace the hand-typed keyframes with human motion generated by
**ARDY** (nv-tlabs/ardy, text-to-motion on the G1 skeleton), which the user chose. Second, give the
engine a way to play a dense clip as one continuous stream, at the clip's own speed.

## Goals

- Four new gestures, **added under new names**. The old ones stay unchanged:
  `wave_natural`, `invite_natural`, `take_right_natural`, `entrance_right_natural` (exact names are
  my call).
- Each plays an ARDY clip continuously at 50 Hz: no stop at each keyframe and no 0.75 rad/s cap
  inside the clip.
- **The user raised the speed ceiling for these clips.** While a clip plays, the overspeed trip comes
  from that clip's own planned peak plus a margin (about 6 rad/s for the wave). Everywhere else it
  stays at the operator's `max_dq` (2.0 on 223).
- The greeter can play the natural set on 223 with the same voice lines.
- Proven in the MuJoCo stand-in first, then the user runs it on 223.

## Non-goals

- Changing the existing gestures, the dual-robot (222) behaviour, or the agent's system prompt.
- Left-side variants (take_left, entrance_left), thanks, bow.
- Running ARDY at runtime: clips are generated offline and committed as data.
- Dex3 finger poses (223 runs `--disable-dex3-hands`).
- Waist or leg motion from ARDY.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it "works")

- Any loosening of safety for the existing gestures, the approach, the way home or stop-retreats.
  Those keep `max_dq`, `PEAK_SPEED`, the leg envelope and the path check exactly as today.
- A stop mid-clip that replays the clip backwards at clip speed.
- A natural gesture that is still slow and stop-start, for example the clip re-quantised into
  min-jerk strokes.

## The clips (decided by the user)

Generated 2026-10-27 with ARDY @ 8163bbf, model `ARDY-G1-RP-25FPS-Horizon52`, 6.0 s,
`--num_samples 4 --seed 0`. The user picked from rendered videos. Files are in
`~/work/g1-demo-gestures/ardy/`: raw CSV/NPZ in `raw/`, picked videos in `videos/`, spike scripts
in `scripts/`, and a README with the exact prompts.

| New gesture | ARDY sample | Speed (user) | Planned peak, approx. |
|---|---|---|---|
| wave_natural | wave_01 (left hand) | 0.7x | ~5.6 rad/s |
| invite_natural | invite_03 | 0.8x | ~3.4 rad/s |
| take_right_natural | take_constrained_01 | 0.8x (my call; the user picked the sample, not the speed) | ~1.8 rad/s |
| entrance_right_natural | entrance_right_01 | 0.8x | ~2.1 rad/s |

- take_constrained_01 used an ARDY right-hand constraint (`raw/take_right_constraint.json`, built
  by `scripts/take_constraint.py`). It pins the right hand at frames 40-70 to the arm pose of
  today's `gestures/take_right.json`, so the reach goes to the same spot (front-right, toward 222's
  bottle). Text-only prompts did not reach there.
- CSV format: MuJoCo qpos at 25 fps, `[root xyz, root quat wxyz, 29 dofs in G1 motor order]`. The
  ARDY joint order matches dimos' motor order (checked against ARDY's `g1.xml`).
- Speed means playback rate: 0.7x stretches the clip to 1/0.7 of its length. `scripts/stretch.py`
  does this with a cubic spline per joint.
- Measured in the spike: the legs barely move in the clips (≤0.5 rad/s) and the root does not
  drift (≤3 cm). The fast joints are shoulder yaw, elbow and wrist roll/yaw.

## How it's built (decided)

1. **Clip data in the repo.** Each gesture gets a data file under `dimos/robot/unitree/g1/gestures/`,
   in a format the builder chooses. It holds both arms only (motors 15-28), already speed-scaled
   and resampled to 50 Hz (`RATE`). It drops ARDY's root, legs and waist. Both arms are kept
   because the "idle" arm moves a little in every clip. `gestures/SOURCE.md` gets a section with
   the provenance from the table above. A small committed converter script regenerates the files
   from the raw CSVs: it must not be collected by pytest, so name it `tool_*.py` or put it outside
   the package. The raw CSVs either go in with it (via LFS if large) or stay in
   `~/work/g1-demo-gestures/ardy/raw/` with SOURCE.md pointing there; builder's call.
2. **Continuous playback.** A gesture plan is: a slow approach from the measured pose to the clip's
   first frame (today's min-jerk stroke under `PEAK_SPEED`), then the clip rows verbatim, then the
   usual slow way home (min-jerk under `PEAK_SPEED`, `return_min_s`). Only the arm joints come from
   the clip. Legs and waist keep the measured base pose, as for every gesture today.
3. **Speed trip per phase.** While the commanded row is inside the clip, the overspeed limit on the
   **arm joints (motors 15-28) only** is one number: the clip's largest planned arm |dq| times a
   margin factor, capped by a hard ceiling constant. The **legs and waist keep `config.max_dq`**
   throughout (my call: the clip never commands them, so a fast leg or waist reaction should still
   trip). The factor and ceiling are set from the stand-in measurements. The starting guess is
   factor 1.2 and ceiling 7.5 rad/s: the reviewer measured the wave's planned peak at 6.06 rad/s
   (left shoulder pitch) after 50 Hz spline resampling, not the ~5.6 measured at 25 fps. During the
   approach, the way home and any retreat, it is `config.max_dq` for every joint, except the plan-tail retreat of item 4, which keeps the clip
   limit through the clip and CLIP_DQ_TAIL_S (0.5 s) after it.
   `SonicArmSkillConfig.max_dq` keeps its `le=5.0` validation; the clip limit is separate and must
   not loosen what the operator sets.
4. **Stop mid-clip (decided by the user: option A).** A `stop_arm` or agent stop that lands while
   the clip is playing lets the clip **play to its end**, then takes the plan's normal slow way home.
   The voice line is still cut at once, as today. No retreat is encoded per clip row and nothing
   reverses through the clip. The stop's report says the clip was finished before returning. A stop
   during the approach behaves as today: it reverses the approach stroke. A stop during the way home
   also behaves as today. **An envelope trip mid-clip finishes the clip like a stop** (user's decision
   2026-10-27, after the MuJoCo stand-in snapped the robot when the trip cancelled at once): the
   clip plays to its end, then the slow way home. Only an overspeed cancels at once (arms above the clip
   limit, legs and waist above `max_dq`), which hands the robot to SONIC's native planner.
   Outside the clip, aborts behave as today. Stop
   latency is therefore at most the rest of the clip (about 3-5 s after trimming); the user
   accepted that.
   Alternatives the user rejected: re-encoding a braking retreat at the stop (~0.5 s latency,
   needs a braking window with a raised limit), and pre-encoding a retreat grid (~15 s of extra
   prepare per act).
5. **Unchanged checks.** The envelope is widened by each joint's largest planned displacement over
   all planned rows. Today `prepare` widens it by the keyframe deltas, which for min-jerk plans is
   the same thing, but it would miss a clip's swing. The both-arm `ArmCollisionChecker` path check runs over every planned row,
   so a clip that brings a hand into the body is refused. The leg envelope uses the existing
   `two_arm` margins and `body_margin`; a natural gesture gets a wider leg margin only if the stand-in
   shows it needs one: the stand-in measured waist_yaw at -0.150 rad in `entrance_right_natural`,
   so it gets `waist_yaw` 0.20 (user, 2026-10-27). The voice line still starts at motion onset minus `voice_lead_s`. Onset keeps
   its current meaning: the first planned movement, so the approach counts. My call: the approach to
   ARDY's standing first frame is short, so this should be close. Retune the lead only if the
   stand-in or 223 shows the line early.
6. **Names and selection.** The four names join `GestureName` and the gesture table, so
   `perform_gesture("wave_natural", "11_wave")` works over MCP. The greeter gets a switch for the
   natural set (my call: an opt-in flag such as `--natural`, keeping today's default). The flag
   implies right-side take and entrance, and refuses `--take-side left` / `--entrance-side left`.
   Pairs: invite_natural+06_invite, take_right_natural+07_take,
   entrance_right_natural+10_entrance, wave_natural+11_wave.
7. **Dual container (222).** The dual path should refuse the natural gestures with a clear message
   unless supporting them falls out for free; builder's call. No 222 runs are planned.

## Validation (decided order)

1. Unit tests: clip loading and joint order, the plan layout (approach, then the clip verbatim, then
   home), the phase-dependent speed limit (arms only, legs and waist at `max_dq`), a stop mid-clip
   playing out then going home (with the voice cut at once), an overspeed mid-clip cancelling at once, an envelope trip mid-clip finishing the clip,
   and the greeter flag.
   Follow the existing tests: `test_imported_gestures.py`, `test_sonic_arm_keyframes.py`,
   `test_sonic_arm_skill.py`, `test_greeter.py`.
2. **MuJoCo stand-in** (`test_sonic_arm_sim_e2e.py` / `sonic_sim_peer`), each natural gesture from
   the stand-in's stance. Pin with `taskset -c 8-15`; the stand-in machine is shared, so check that
   no other session is running a sim first. Record per gesture: arm tracking (how much of the clip's
   amplitude SONIC actually reproduces), peak measured |dq| against the clip limit, legs
   max_deviation, foot contacts, tilt. Pass: completes without an abort, legs within margin, ≥7
   floor contacts unless a case floor is justified (as with entrance_right's 6), and the wave
   visibly a wave at roughly clip speed.
3. Render the stand-in runs to video for the user to judge naturalness before any hardware run.
4. **Hardware:** the user runs 223 (an operator action). The run command is the one in the
   `g1-gesture-voice-223` runbook, then `perform_gesture` per gesture, then the greeter with the flag.

## Left to the builder

- The clip file format, the converter's name and location, and whether the raw CSVs are committed.
- How the dense segment fits `KeyframePlan` / `Segment` / `retreat_route` (for example a new segment
  kind, or a separate clip plan type), and how the chunker and token schedule handle it. The longest
  clip plus approach and return is about 15 s, under the 20 s session chunk.
- Trimming the clips' trailing stillness (ARDY's 6 s holds ~2-3 s of standing after each act),
  keeping ≥0.5 s of settle.
- The speed-limit factor and ceiling after the stand-in measurements, reported back to the user if
  they differ from 1.25 and 7.0.
- Exact gesture names.

## Risks

- SONIC may not track 3-6 rad/s arm motion: the motion would be clipped in amplitude or lag, and
  the legs could react. The stand-in measures this first. If tracking is poor, the fallback is a
  slower playback factor, which needs the user's OK.
- The measured |dq| on hardware is noisier than in sim, so a single noisy sample could trip the clip
  limit. Report it if seen. A debounce was never requested and isn't part of this design.
- The clip's first frame may differ noticeably from 223's measured stance. The approach stroke
  covers that, at today's slow speed.
